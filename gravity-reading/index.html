8.3 Lesson 10 — Gravity: The Reading

Paragraph 1.

Gravity is a force at a distance. Any two objects with mass pull on each other. The objects do not have to touch. Gravity only pulls. It never pushes.

Paragraph 2.

The pull depends on mass. The more mass the objects have, the stronger the pull between them. Earth has an enormous mass, so Earth's pull on you is strong. The pull between small objects is real too, but it is far too weak to feel.

Paragraph 3.

The pull also depends on distance. The farther apart the two objects are, the weaker the pull between them. The pull drops off fast. When the distance doubles, the pull drops to one fourth of what it was.

Paragraph 4.

Near Earth, gravity pulls everything toward the center of Earth. That is why objects fall down when you drop them. It is also why you stay on the ground instead of floating away.

Paragraph 5.

Mass and weight are not the same thing. Mass is the amount of matter in an object. An object's mass stays the same wherever it goes. Weight is gravity's pull on that mass.

Paragraph 6.

The Moon has less mass than Earth, so the Moon's gravity is weaker than Earth's. On the Moon, your weight would be much less than it is on Earth. Your mass would be exactly the same.

Paragraph 7.

Gravity and magnetic force belong to the same family: forces at a distance. Neither force needs the objects to touch, and both forces get weaker as distance grows. But they are not the same force. A magnet can push or pull, and it acts only on some materials. Gravity only pulls, and it acts between any two objects that have mass.
